## Sovereignty buys the growth; the market is told to deliver it

Europe's space money is growing because it has become defence money, while the delivery model is being rebuilt around competing private firms. The two logics want different things from the same suppliers.

Budgets rose 12% to €13.5 billion on the back of German defence spending [european-space-budgets-up-12-to-13-5bn], and 80% of institutional upstream demand is now defence-driven [defence-drives-80-of-institutional-demand]. IRIS² added a dedicated 66-satellite defence layer to a 348-satellite sovereign constellation [iris2-grows-to-348-plus-66-defence-satellites]. At the same time ESA is buying launch as a service from four competing challengers with 40% private co-funding [launcher-challenge-signs-frames-loses-orbex], a new-space manufacturer took the largest IRIS² platform order [aerospacelab-wins-2-4bn-iris2-leo-prime], and private capital is at a record [european-spacetech-vc-hits-2bn-in-h1-2026]. A sovereign buyer wants control, security accreditation and geographic return; a market wants cadence, price and the freedom to sell abroad. Orbex's exit shows the market side can fail fast; the defence side cannot afford that.

## The orbits Europe is buying are the ones degrading fastest

Europe is committing to fill higher LEO in the exact year ESA reports the environment there worsening by an order of magnitude.

The Space Environment Index jumped from ~4 to ~50 in a year and ESA cut its collision-projection horizon in half [space-environment-index-jumps-from-4-to-50]. IRIS² will put 414 satellites into higher LEO from 2029 [iris2-grows-to-348-plus-66-defence-satellites] and Ariane 6's manifest is anchored by Amazon's megaconstellation [amazon-leo-lifts-ariane-6-order-to-24]. Europe's answer is normative — 200 charter signatories [zero-debris-charter-passes-200-signatories] and a Space Act whose sustainability rules apply from 2030 [eu-space-act-draft-report-tabled-in-itre] — which is to say, after the constellations are designed. The tension is between an environmental fact moving on a one-year clock and a regulatory response on a five-year one.

## Brussels writes the rules; the capitals hold the money and the security clause

The EU is claiming competence over space activity through the Space Act while the growth is national, defence-led and outside EU competence.

The Act's committee stage began in March 2026 with adoption not expected before 2028 [eu-space-act-draft-report-tabled-in-itre]; the national-security carve-out is the contested clause. Meanwhile the money that grew is German defence money [european-space-budgets-up-12-to-13-5bn], the buyer is a ministry of defence [defence-drives-80-of-institutional-demand], and IRIS²'s defence layer is a Commission programme serving national security users [iris2-grows-to-348-plus-66-defence-satellites]. ESA — neither EU nor national — runs the launcher competition [launcher-challenge-signs-frames-loses-orbex]. Three governance layers, one industrial base.

## Every plan hires the same five thousand engineers

The constraint that none of the growth signals name is the one ESA has started to measure.

IRIS² needs 264 satellites from one company by 2030 [aerospacelab-wins-2-4bn-iris2-leo-prime], four launchers must reach orbit by 2027 [launcher-challenge-signs-frames-loses-orbex] with a first success already logged [isar-spectrum-reaches-orbit-from-and-ya], Ariane 6 must double its cadence [amazon-leo-lifts-ariane-6-order-to-24], and $4bn of venture money is looking for teams [european-spacetech-vc-hits-2bn-in-h1-2026]. ESA's Skills Digital Twin exists because systems, propulsion, flight-software, AOCS and RF engineers are already the binding constraint [esa-skills-digital-twin-maps-sector-gaps]. Debt for mid-caps [eib-space-techeu-puts-500m-behind-smes] buys machines, not people. The tension is between capital that arrived in two years and a workforce that takes ten.
