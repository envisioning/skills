## Three things that changed

**1. The customer changed.** European public space budgets grew 12% to €13.5 billion in 2025 — the first double-digit year in five — and the growth is German defence spending [european-space-budgets-up-12-to-13-5bn]. Eighty percent of institutional upstream demand is now defence [defence-drives-80-of-institutional-demand]. IRIS² was re-scoped in August to 348 satellites plus a separate 66-satellite defence layer, first launches 2029 [iris2-grows-to-348-plus-66-defence-satellites]. The European space economy of the next decade is, in the main, a security economy.

**2. The supplier base changed.** A Belgian company founded in 2018 won the largest platform order in IRIS², €2.4 billion for 264 satellites [aerospacelab-wins-2-4bn-iris2-leo-prime]. Isar Aerospace reached orbit from Norway on 5 September [isar-spectrum-reaches-orbit-from-and-ya]. ESA's Launcher Challenge is now four firms with over €900 million and a 2027 deadline, after Orbex went into administration [launcher-challenge-signs-frames-loses-orbex]. Venture funding hit $2 billion in six months, more than all of 2025 [european-spacetech-vc-hits-2bn-in-h1-2026], and the EIB opened a €500 million debt programme for the mid-caps VC ignores [eib-space-techeu-puts-500m-behind-smes]. Ariane 6's order book is full — Amazon alone has 24 launches [amazon-leo-lifts-ariane-6-order-to-24] — so the question is cadence, not demand.

**3. The environment changed faster than the rules.** ESA's Space Environment Index worsened by an order of magnitude in one year, from about 4 to 50, and ESA halved its collision-projection horizon [space-environment-index-jumps-from-4-to-50]. Europe's response is the Zero Debris Charter, now past 200 signatories [zero-debris-charter-passes-200-signatories], and the EU Space Act, in committee since March with adoption not expected before 2028 and application from 2030 [eu-space-act-draft-report-tabled-in-itre] — after the constellations above are designed.

## What it means

Four tensions, detailed in the [frame set](./frames-tensions.md):

- **Sovereignty buys the growth; the market is told to deliver it.** Defence wants control and geographic return; challengers and new primes want cadence and export freedom. Same suppliers.
- **The orbits Europe is buying are the ones degrading fastest.** A one-year environmental clock against a five-year regulatory one.
- **Brussels writes the rules; the capitals hold the money and the security clause.** Three governance layers — EU, ESA, national — on one industrial base.
- **Every plan hires the same engineers.** Capital arrived in two years; the workforce takes ten. ESA has started measuring the gap [esa-skills-digital-twin-maps-sector-gaps].

For a participatory scan, the second and fourth tensions are the ones a room can add evidence to that this scan lacks: the corpus is thin on Earth observation, navigation, and on the workforce itself.
