# Adjacency Scan

> Ported from the Signals AI playbook `adjacency-scan`. The same structure runs at scale in [Signals AI](https://signals.envisioning.com).

Explores where to play next — adjacent categories, customers, geographies, channels, and capabilities — relative to a defined home position. Grounded in industry filings, market data, product landscapes, and reputable trade press.

- **Audience:** Corporate strategy, corporate development, and product
- **Use when:** Mapping adjacent moves available from a defined home position before committing to a new bet.

## Boundary

Identify concrete adjacent moves — neighbouring categories, customers, geographies, channels, or capabilities — reachable from the **home position** defined for this run. Stay **inside that one-hop boundary**; do not surface unrelated diversification ideas. Skip moves that lack a defensible public signal that the adjacency is real and active.

## What counts as a signal here

Each item is a **specific adjacent opportunity** anchored in evidence — a category with named players, a customer segment with measured demand, a geography with documented traction, a channel in active use elsewhere, a capability with named exemplars.

## Categories (pick exactly one)

| category | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Adjacent Category | Neighbouring product or service categories the business could enter from its current base. |
| Adjacent Customer | Customer segments, buyer types, or use occasions one step removed from today's core. |
| Adjacent Geography | Markets, regions, or jurisdictions reachable from current operations with bounded incremental effort. |
| Adjacent Channel | Routes to market, distribution, or partnership structures not currently used. |
| Adjacent Capability | Capabilities, IP, or operating muscles a short hop from existing strengths that unlock new options. |

## Metrics (score every signal on each)

### Strategic Fit (`strategic-fit`)

How well the adjacency aligns with the defined home position's strengths and posture.

1 · Poor · 2 · Weak · 3 · Plausible · 4 · Strong · 5 · Native

### Defensibility (`defensibility`)

How protectable a position in this adjacency could become against incumbents and new entrants.

1 · Open field · 2 · Contested · 3 · Moderate · 4 · Defensible · 5 · Structural

### Time to Entry (`time-to-entry`)

How quickly a credible position could be established in the adjacency from the current base.

1 · Immediate · 2 · Within months · 3 · 1–2 years · 4 · 2–5 years · 5 · Beyond 5 years

## Sourcing

Search the web for credible sources that substantiate or refute this adjacency signal **from the stated home position**. Prefer industry reports with methodology, company filings with segment disclosure, named-player product or pricing pages, peer-reviewed market research, and reputable trade press. Avoid generic consulting marketing, broad market-size pages without methodology, and AI-generated overview pages.

## Merging

Merge near-duplicate adjacency signals that point to the same one-hop opportunity from the home position. Prefer the title that names the adjacency specifically and avoids consulting framing. When merging, preserve the stronger named-evidence hook in the summary.

## Defaults

- signals per category: 5
- sources per signal: up to 5
